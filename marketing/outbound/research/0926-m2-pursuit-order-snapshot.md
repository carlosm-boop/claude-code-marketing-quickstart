## Sequencing cohorts — one ICP, one trigger, one offer

The C1 guardrails require one ICP per campaign, one trigger per sequence and one offer per sequence. A ranked pursuit order can hold mixed triggers because a person reads it and decides; a sequence roster cannot. These cohorts are the sequencing view of the same 29 accounts — each account sits in exactly one, and no account enters a sequence from more than one trigger.

## How to read the evidence strip

Eight ICP-M2 gates per account: **VER** industry on the ICP list · **AGE** founded 2018 or earlier · **SCL** late-stage/PE scale · **VOL** company-side transaction volume · **EST** named estate or database pain · **MRG** margin-scrutiny trigger · **HIR** infra hiring live · **ACC** production access grantable.

`●` evidence in hand · `◐` partial or inferred · `○` no evidence either way · `✕` known blocker

Rank weights **EST** and **MRG** heaviest — those two predict a meeting. Deliberately no composite score.

## Removed, and why

**Suppliers, not prospects** — Temporal · CData · Port.io · Hydrolix · Fluidstack · Skyflow · CloudLinux · Tines · WEKA

Infrastructure and developer-platform vendors. Temporal is the clearest case — a $754M durable-execution company whose customers *are* ICP-M2. These are partner or competitor conversations, and they carry the deepest in-house platform benches in the set. The banded run added two more: WEKA is an AI storage system, and Tines is workflow and AI orchestration — the same category as Temporal, which it correctly excluded earlier.

**No single estate, no single CTO** — Red Ventures

A portfolio holding company of independently operated media brands — Bankrate, Lonely Planet, The Points Guy. Its volume is traffic and affiliate referrals, not transactions, and no one owns a single margin question. Portfolio estate consolidation is a real sale, but to a different buyer.

**Wrong deployment model** — Litera

Twenty-five years old and an Hg-owned serial acquirer, so the acquisition-driven sprawl is genuine. But it is document software wired into Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace — 2.3 million daily users there does not indicate a high-volume cloud database estate. Closer to M1 than M2.

**No buying committee** — Weights & Biases

Acquired by CoreWeave for roughly $1.7B, deal closed. No CTO there personally owns a margin question any more, and selling cloud-cost optimisation into a company owned by a cloud provider runs backwards.

**Public or SPAC-bound** — WeRide · PlusAI · ACV Auctions · Angi (listed as “Handy HQ”)

WeRide is listed on both NASDAQ and HKEX; PlusAI is merging with Churchill Capital IX. ICP-M2 stops at pre-IPO, and autonomy estates are GPU and data-lake shaped rather than transactional. The banded run then leaked two more: ACV Auctions trades as NASDAQ:ACVA yet came through tagged Series E, and the row labelled “Handy HQ” on angi.com is Angi (NASDAQ:ANGI) carrying Handy's pre-acquisition funding — an unflagged name/domain mismatch of exactly the kind Origami caught for Whatfix and HiBob.

**No MVP era to modernise** — Vertice · Hebbia · Wispr Flow · Rillet · Accrete · Ambient.ai · David AI · Unify · Labelbox · Vanta · osapiens · Atlan

Founded 2019 or later. “MVP-era architecture now taxing margins” is a claim about elapsed time; greenfield infrastructure has nothing to unwind. Vertice was founded in 2021 and still passed at 479 employees, which is the age gate's absence made visible.

**Production access is a non-starter** — Peregrine

Government and public-safety data platform. Assessment-by-hearsay fails, and this is an explicit ICP disqualifier rather than a difficulty to negotiate.

**Estate too small or wrong shape** — Brainly · First Due · Kantata · Envoy · LumApps · Uberall · Trackforce · Luminance · onXmaps · Laravel · Vimeo · Intercom · Showpad · Salsify · Fullstory · Mural · Simpplr · Accela · H1

Records-management and workplace software at low write volume, or document-processing estates where per-cluster database savings cannot reach the size that funds a program. Brainly fails the industry qualifier on the word that matters: its volume is read traffic and content queries, not transactions — there is no order, payment, policy or booking in it. Consumer content platforms are cache- and CDN-shaped, so neither M2 proof point transfers, and Prosus-backed Brainly has already been through its efficiency cycles. Vimeo and Intercom are the other interesting rejections: both carry genuine volume, but video delivery is object-storage and CDN shaped and customer-service messaging is horizontal rather than vertical SaaS.

## Where sourcing goes next

1. Stop ranking by headcount. Every run has returned its largest companies under the ceiling and nothing else. Screening must report in bands — 200–600, 600–1,200, 1,200–2,500 — with a qualified count per band, so fit density across the range is visible.
1. Screen the remaining 478. At the observed 27% qualify rate that implies roughly 130 qualified accounts in the full pool, which matches the earlier 60–150 estimate and is enough to size the campaign properly.
1. Drop revenue entirely. Coverage across three runs was 0%, 37%, 0%. It is dead weight that silently excludes qualified accounts.
1. Promote transaction volume to the binding gate. Company-side counts only — orders, payments, policies, cards, bookings. Customer assets under management and spend under management are not evidence of a database estate, and treating them as such is what admitted Carta's $185B, Pave, Hebbia's “$30T AUM” and Vertice's “$30bn in spend”.
1. Hold the qualified universe as a watchlist, not a campaign list. Monitor *it* for hiring triggers continuously. A trigger on a pre-qualified account is a send; a trigger on an unqualified account is noise, which is what the first run mostly was.
