## 0. THE PROOF FIREWALL — non-negotiable

**Client names and performance metrics must NEVER appear together, in any asset.**

- **Pool A (names)** — broad trust strip only: "companies we've worked with." **No numbers, outcomes, or specifics attached.**
- **Pool B (metrics)** — always anonymized by industry ("a national rail operator," "a global travel-technology provider"). **Never tied to a name.**
- **Never join them.** Do not state or imply which named client produced a given result, even where it is inferable from public record.
- Names render as **text**, not logo images, unless per-client usage clearance and an approved asset kit both exist.

**Why:** the case-study material is anonymized for contractual reasons. Joining the pools re-identifies clients and creates real exposure. This applies to blog posts, LinkedIn, cold email, landing pages, and AEO content — not just decks.

**If a draft needs a named-client proof point, the correct move is to escalate, not to improvise.** See [`../rules/escalation.md`](../rules/escalation.md).

**Confirmed policy (Rudra, 2026-08-31).** This is settled, not a working assumption. The older positioning foundation pairs names with metrics (HoneyQuote / SNCF / Amadeus); that pairing is retired. Use [`../messaging/messaging.md`](../messaging/messaging.md) §6.

**Adding a named customer is a human act.** If WeKan wants to name a customer against an outcome, Rudra adds those details himself after clearing them. No skill or agent ever proposes the pairing, infers it, or asks for permission to make it.

## 1. Write to a CTO, not a marketer

**The rule:** Assume the reader is a senior technical decision-maker with a legacy estate and a board asking about AI.

**Violation:** "Unlock the transformative power of AI to supercharge your digital journey."
**Fix:** "You cannot operationalize intelligence on an architecture that can't move. Modernization comes first."

## 2. Lead with the insight, not the company

**The rule:** The first line earns attention with a point of view. WeKan enters after the reader already agrees with something.

**Violation:** "WeKan is a global technology consulting company specialized in…"
**Fix:** "Access to models is commoditized. Operationalizing them is the hard part — and that's where most enterprise AI programs stall."

## 3. Specific over sweeping

**The rule:** Every claim names a mechanism, a number, or a constraint. If it could be said by any SI, cut it.

**Violation:** "We deliver faster with AI."
**Fix:** "Seven specialized agents run the modernization — Discovery through Validation — which is what gets timelines ~50% shorter with under 1% migration defects."

## 4. Own the arc, never a fragment

**The rule:** Anything that positions WeKan as a point solution is a voice violation, not just a messaging one.

**Violation:** "WeKan: your database modernization partner."
**Fix:** "Modernization is the on-ramp, not the destination. WeKan owns the arc through to agentic operations."

## 5. Confident, never breathless

**The rule:** Senior, declarative, unhurried. No hype punctuation, no emoji, no "revolutionary" / "game-changing" / "unleash."

**Violation:** "This changes EVERYTHING for enterprise AI!!"
**Fix:** "This is an architectural shift, not an incremental one — different in kind, not degree."

**Exception:** founder-voice LinkedIn posts may run looser and more personal, but still never use emoji or hype punctuation.

## 6. Evidence-bound

**The rule:** Never invent a metric, customer, or capability. If a number isn't in `messaging.md` §6 or `company.md`, write `[UNAVAILABLE]` and flag it.

Pairs with [`../../.claude/rules/evidence-bound-outputs.md`](../../.claude/rules/evidence-bound-outputs.md).
